## 1. Break-Even Mathematics in Commercial Publishing

### Q: What is the fundamental mathematical definition of break-even in business underwriting, and how does it apply to book publishing?

**A:** In managerial accounting and capital underwriting, break-even analysis identifies the operating volume at which total revenues exactly equal total costs (both fixed and variable), resulting in zero operating profit ($\pi = 0$).

In publishing economics:

1. **Fixed Costs ($F$):** One-off capital expenditures required to produce the book that do not scale with unit sales volume (e.g., developmental editing, copyediting, cover illustration, typesetting, interior formatting, ISBN registration).
2. **Variable Production/Distribution Costs ($C$):** Direct per-unit manufacturing and fulfillment expenses incurred on every sale (e.g., print-on-demand printing cost for physical paperbacks/hardcovers, digital delivery bandwidth fees for ebooks).
3. **Gross Royalty ($R$):** The contractual or platform royalty earned by the author/publisher per copy sold from the retail list price ($P$).
4. **Unit Contribution Margin ($U$):** The residual operating cash flow generated by selling one copy:
   $$U = R - C$$
5. **Operating Profit Equation:**
   $$\pi(Q) = Q \times R - (F + Q \times C) = Q \times (R - C) - F = Q \times U - F$$
6. **Break-Even Condition:** Setting $\pi(Q) = 0$ yields:
   $$Q \times U = F \implies Q = \frac{F}{U}$$

Because books are physical or discrete digital items that can only be sold in whole integer units ($Q \in \mathbb{N}_0$), the true operational break-even point is the minimal integer $Q$ such that operating profit is non-negative ($\pi(Q) \ge 0$), which requires the ceiling operation:
$$Q_{\text{be}} = \left\lceil \frac{F}{U} \right\rceil$$

## 2. Exact Integer Ceiling Division without Floating-Point Math

### Q: Why is `Math.ceil()` on floating-point numbers forbidden in financial systems, and how is exact integer ceiling division implemented with BigInt?

**A:** JavaScript `Number` represents values using IEEE 754 double-precision 64-bit binary floating-point. Binary floats cannot exactly represent decimal fractions whose denominators contain prime factors other than 2. For instance, $0.1$ and $0.2$ are recurring binary fractions.

In financial calculations, floating-point inaccuracies cause catastrophic rounding errors when evaluating ceilings:

1. **False Remainder Issue:** Suppose exact division should yield an integer, e.g. $1000 / 200 = 5.0$. If floating-point arithmetic evaluates the fraction as $5.000000000000001$, `Math.ceil()` rounds it up to $6$, introducing an off-by-one error.
2. **Premature Truncation Issue:** If a division evaluates to $4.999999999999999$ due to binary representation limits, `Math.ceil()` yields $5$ instead of the correct higher integer.

**Pure BigInt Ceiling Algorithm:**
Given integer minor-unit fixed cost $F$ and unit contribution expressed as an exact rational fraction $\frac{U_{\text{num}}}{U_{\text{den}}}$ (where $F \ge 0, U_{\text{num}} > 0, U_{\text{den}} > 0$):
$$\frac{F}{U} = \frac{F}{U_{\text{num}} / U_{\text{den}}} = \frac{F \times U_{\text{den}}}{U_{\text{num}}}$$
We perform integer division and remainder using native `BigInt`:
$$\text{Numerator} = BigInt(F) \times BigInt(U_{\text{den}})$$
$$\text{Denominator} = BigInt(U_{\text{num}})$$
$$\text{quotient} = \text{Numerator} / \text{Denominator}$$
$$\text{remainder} = \text{Numerator} \% \text{Denominator}$$

The ceiling decision is strictly binary:

- If $\text{remainder} === 0n$: The division is exact; $Q_{\text{be}} = \text{quotient}$.
- If $\text{remainder} > 0n$: Fractional remainder exists; $Q_{\text{be}} = \text{quotient} + 1n$.

This algorithm is 100% deterministic, arbitrary-precision, and has zero floating-point dependencies.

## 3. Contribution Margin versus Retail Revenue

### Q: What is the difference between unit contribution and retail revenue, and why must break-even units be derived from contribution rather than revenue?

**A:**

- **Retail Revenue ($P$):** The gross price paid by the end reader (e.g., \$14.99 for a paperback). The publisher does NOT receive this entire amount. The retailer (Amazon, Barnes & Noble, Ingram) takes a distribution share (e.g., 40%), leaving a gross royalty of $60\%$ (\$8.99).
- **Unit Contribution ($U$):** The actual net operating cash retained by the author/publisher after deducting manufacturing costs from the royalty ($U = R - C$).
  For example, if print cost is \$4.52:
  $$U = \$8.99 - \$4.52 = \$4.47$$
- **Why Contribution Governs Break-Even:**
  If an author has \$2,000 in fixed editing costs, using retail revenue to calculate break-even ($2000 / 14.99 = 134$ units) would be disastrously misleading. At 134 units, the author collects only $134 \times \$4.47 = \$598.98$ of net cash flow, falling short of covering the \$2,000 cost by \$1,401.02.
  Using contribution:
  $$Q_{\text{be}} = \lceil 2000 / 4.47 \rceil = 448 \text{ units}$$
  Selling 448 books yields $448 \times \$4.47 = \$2,002.56$, fully covering the \$2,000 capital investment.

## 4. Break-Even Units versus Break-Even Revenue

### Q: How is break-even revenue defined in Nimphy, what is its precise semantic meaning, and why was it chosen over continuous formulas?

**A:** In standard cost accounting textbooks, break-even sales revenue is often presented as a continuous ratio:
$\text{Revenue}^* = \frac{\text{FixedCost}}{\text{Contribution Margin Ratio}} = \frac{F}{U / P}$
However, in Nimphy, break-even revenue is approved per repository owner Decision 2 as:
$\text{Revenue}_{\text{be}} = \text{listPriceMinor} \times Q_{\text{be}}$

**Precise Semantic Meaning:**
**Gross retail sales revenue at the whole-unit, formula-defined break-even threshold.**

It is critical to distinguish this semantic definition:

1. **Not Author Royalties:** Gross retail revenue is the total dollar volume paid by retail readers. It does NOT represent the author's royalties or net disbursements.
2. **Not Publisher Proceeds or Net Profit:** Distribution channels take retail discounts; gross revenue is not net publisher cash flow.
3. **Ledger Consistency with Scenario Totals:** Consistent with the scenario engine (where $\text{revenue} = \text{units} \times \text{listPriceMinor}$), calculating revenue at the discrete whole-unit break-even quantity ensures that an author running a scenario at $Q_{\text{be}}$ observes identical revenue across cards.
4. **Unreachable Calculations:** For unreachable calculations (zero or negative contribution), both `units` and `revenue` are strictly omitted. Unreachable is never represented as zero units or zero revenue.

## 5. Unreachable Cases: Zero and Negative Unit Contribution

### Q: Under what financial conditions is break-even unreachable, and how does Nimphy prevent misleading outputs?

**A:**

1. **Zero Contribution ($U = 0$):**
   When per-unit gross royalty exactly equals manufacturing cost ($R = C$), selling additional books generates \$0.00 of margin. No matter how many books are sold, fixed costs can never be recovered.
2. **Negative Contribution ($U < 0$):**
   When per-unit manufacturing cost exceeds gross royalty ($C > R$), every sale produces an incremental loss. Increasing sales volume accelerates financial loss rather than amortizing fixed costs. (e.g., selling a 300-page paperback for \$5.00 where royalty is \$3.00 and printing cost is \$6.52 yields $-\$3.52$ per copy).

**Type-Safe Handling:**
Rather than returning magic numbers (`units: -1`, `units: null`, or `units: 0`), Nimphy models `BreakEvenResult` as a discriminated union:

```typescript
export type BreakEvenResult =
  | { readonly reachable: true; readonly units: number; readonly revenue: Money; ... }
  | { readonly reachable: false; readonly unreachableReason: 'ZERO_CONTRIBUTION' | 'NEGATIVE_CONTRIBUTION'; ... }
```

When `reachable === false`, `units` and `revenue` are strictly omitted from the type.

**Zero Fixed Cost Boundary:**
If fixed cost is zero ($F = 0$) but unit contribution is negative ($U < 0$), the result must remain `reachable: false`. Selling copies still incurs an operational loss. A negative-margin book is never "at break-even" merely because zero capital was invested upfront.

## 6. Fixed-Cost Modeling and Monorepo Architecture

### Q: Why is `fixedCost` passed as an explicit `Money` parameter to `calculateBreakEven` rather than added to `ScenarioParameters` or the Rule Pack?

**A:**

1. **Separation of Concerns (Doc 03, ADR-001):**
   `ScenarioParameters` (FR-006) models market sales sensitivity (`units`, `listPriceMinor`, `costVariancePercent`). Fixed costs belong to the business case's capital structure (editing, design, marketing), not the sales volume parameters of an illustrative scenario.
2. **Rule Pack Immutability (ADR-006):**
   Rule Packs represent official, platform-wide publisher economics (e.g., Amazon KDP printing fees, Apple Books royalty tiers). Fixed costs are user-specific and have no place in platform rule packs.
3. **Database & API Decoupling:**
   Keeping `fixedCost` as an explicit parameter in the pure calculation engine allows callers (such as API use-cases in STEP-013) to aggregate a user's multi-item fixed cost list (`fixedCosts: [{ label: "Editing", value: Money }]`) and supply the sum to `calculateBreakEven` without polluting domain scenario types.

## 7. Safe Integer Overflow Protection

### Q: How does the calculation engine protect against integer overflow in high-magnitude financial scenarios?

**A:** JavaScript safe integers are bounded by:
$$\text{Number.MAX\_SAFE\_INTEGER} = 2^{53} - 1 = 9,007,199,254,740,991$$
While intermediate arithmetic uses arbitrary-precision `BigInt`, converting final values back to JavaScript `number` (for `units` and `Money.amountMinor`) can lose precision if unvalidated.

Nimphy applies explicit pre-conversion guards:

```typescript
if (breakEvenUnitsBig > BigInt(Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER)) {
  throw new CalcOverflowError(
    `Break-even units calculation overflowed safe integer bounds: ${breakEvenUnitsBig}`,
  );
}
```

And on retail revenue:

```typescript
const revenueBig = BigInt(listPriceMinor) * breakEvenUnitsBig;
if (revenueBig > BigInt(Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER)) {
  throw new CalcOverflowError(`Break-even revenue calculation overflowed safe integer bounds`);
}
```

This guarantees fail-closed behavior with typed, actionable errors rather than silent numerical corruption.

## 8. Currency Consistency & Boundary Verification

### Q: How does Nimphy enforce currency safety across multi-currency operations?

**A:** In accordance with Principle P-05 (Verify at the boundary) and ADR-005, Nimphy forbids implicit cross-currency arithmetic:

1. `validateBreakEvenInput` checks that `fixedCost.currency` matches `input.currency`. If they differ, it immediately throws `CalcCurrencyMismatchError`.
2. It verifies that `input.currency` matches `rulePack.currency`. If they differ, it throws `CalcCurrencyMismatchError`.
3. All intermediate values and output `Money` instances preserve the validated ISO-4217 currency code.

## 9. ADR-005 Rounding Point Interaction: `per_unit` versus `per_total` and Rounding Mode Investigation

### Q: How does the break-even calculation interact with `per_unit` versus `per_total`, what did the rounding-mode investigation establish, and why was the roadmap formula retained without a ledger search or `Q_be - 1` adjustment?

**A:** Per repository owner Decision 1, Nimphy retains the explicitly specified roadmap formula:
$Q_{\text{be}} = \left\lceil \frac{\text{FixedCost}}{\text{ExactUnitContribution}} \right\rceil$
using exact rational arithmetic and BigInt ceiling division.

**Critical Architectural Distinction:**
The system explicitly distinguishes:

1. **The formula-defined break-even quantity** required by the roadmap.
2. **The minimum quantity that happens to cover fixed cost** under independently rounded aggregate ledger components.

We do NOT claim that the formula-defined quantity is always the minimum rounded-ledger threshold.

**Mandatory Rounding-Mode Investigation Findings:**

- **Schema Validation Inspection:** Source inspection of `packages/rules/src/schema/types.ts` and `packages/rules/src/schema/validator.ts` confirms that `ROUNDING_MODES` and `ROUNDING_POINTS` are validated independently. The architecture permits all four rounding modes (`round_half_up`, `round_half_even`, `round_down`, `round_up`) with `per_total`.
- **Integer-Shift Property Analysis:**
  For non-negative aggregate amounts ($R, C \ge 0$) and an integer fixed cost $F \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, exact rational coverage requires $R - C \ge F \iff R \ge C + F$.
  Under independent aggregate ledger rounding, rounded coverage requires $\text{round}(R) - \text{round}(C) \ge F$.
  Rounding modes that preserve the **integer-shift property** ($\text{round}(x + F) = \text{round}(x) + F$ for integer $F$) guarantee coverage:
  - `round_down` ($\lfloor x \rfloor$ on non-negative values): $\lfloor x + F \rfloor = \lfloor x \rfloor + F$, which guarantees $\lfloor R \rfloor - \lfloor C \rfloor \ge F$.
  - `round_up` ($\lceil x \rceil$ on non-negative values): $\lceil x + F \rceil = \lceil x \rceil + F$, which guarantees $\lceil R \rceil - \lceil C \rceil \ge F$.
  - `round_half_up` ($\lfloor x + 0.5 \rfloor$ on non-negative values): $\lfloor (x + F) + 0.5 \rfloor = \lfloor x + 0.5 \rfloor + F$, preserving coverage.
- **Demonstrated Counterexample with `round_half_even`:**
  `round_half_even` (Banker's rounding) is the **demonstrated exception** because tie-breaking depends on the parity of the integer part $\lfloor x \rfloor$. Adding an odd integer fixed cost $F$ flips the parity of the integer part, causing half-integer ties to break in opposite directions:
  - Exact royalty: 2.5
  - Exact cost: 1.5
  - Exact contribution: 1.0
  - Fixed cost: 1 (odd integer)
  - Formula-defined quantity: $Q_{\text{be}} = \lceil 1 / 1 \rceil = 1$.
  - At $Q = 1$, scenario ledger aggregation under ADR-005:
    - Rounded aggregate royalty: `round_half_even(2.5)` = 2 (rounds down to even)
    - Rounded aggregate cost: `round_half_even(1.5)` = 2 (rounds up to even)
    - Rounded aggregate contribution: $2 - 2 = 0$.
    - Under-covers fixed cost: $0 < 1$.
  - At $Q = 2$, aggregate royalty rounds to 5, cost rounds to 3, contribution = 2 $\ge 1$.
  - Therefore, the minimum quantity covering fixed cost in the rounded ledger is 2, while the formula-defined threshold is 1.
- **Exact Corrected Conclusions:**
  1. `round_down` must NOT be classified as an unsafe mode based on the `round_half_even` counterexample.
  2. Disallowing `round_down` without a valid independent counterexample is unjustified and rejected.
  3. The current Rule Pack schema permits all four rounding modes with `per_total`.
  4. Neither the Rule Pack schema, validator, ADR-005, nor financial calculation implementation has been changed.
  5. Universal ledger coverage across all possible configurations is not claimed unless formally established.

**Why Ledger-Minimum Search and `Q_be - 1` Adjustment were Explicitly Rejected:**

- The repository owner decided not to replace the roadmap formula with a ledger-minimum search in STEP-007.
- The proposed `Q_be - 1` adjustment was rejected because aggregate rounded contribution under `per_total` can be non-monotonic; checking only the immediately preceding quantity does not establish true minimum-unit optimality and can miss earlier qualifying quantities.
- Neither ADR-005, the rounding policy, nor the roadmap formula was silently changed.

## 10. Deterministic Audit Trace and Immutability (P-03, P-04)

### Q: How does the break-even engine satisfy the traceability and evidence immutability principles?

**A:**

1. **Deterministic Calculation Trace:**
   Every execution of `calculateBreakEven` constructs a structured `BreakEvenCalculationTrace` capturing:
   - `engineVersion: '0.1.0'`
   - `rulePackRef`: pack ID, pack key, version, SHA-256 content hash.
   - `input`: fixed cost, currency, list price, format, page count, file size, cost variance.
   - `unitContribution`: rounding point, rounding mode, unrounded numerator/denominator, decimal string, rounded minor units.
   - `division`: exact numerator, denominator, quotient, remainder, ceiling decision.
   - `result`: reachable status, units, revenue.
   - `steps`: sequential chronological execution steps.
2. **Zero Nondeterministic Fields:**
   The trace contains zero timestamps (`new Date()`) and zero random UUIDs (`crypto.randomUUID()`). Serializing the trace to canonical JSON via `@nimphy/domain`'s `toCanonicalJson` yields bit-for-bit identical bytes across repeated runs.
3. **Deep Immutability:**
   Before returning, `deepFreeze` recursively freezes the result object and all nested properties and arrays, preventing downstream callers from tampering with calculated financial evidence.

## 11. Property-Based Testing with Seeded PRNGs

### Q: How was property-based testing utilized to verify the mathematical invariants of break-even economics?

**A:** Rather than testing only a handful of hardcoded inputs, Nimphy uses deterministic property-based testing with a seeded Mulberry32 pseudo-random number generator to verify algebraic invariants across random parameter spaces:

1. **Fixed-Cost Monotonicity:** $F_2 \ge F_1 \implies Q_{\text{be}}(F_2) \ge Q_{\text{be}}(F_1)$.
2. **Threshold Coverage under Supported Synthetic Packs:** For any reachable result with $F > 0$ across the repository's synthetic rule packs (which use `per_unit` or `per_total` with `round_half_up`), net contribution evaluated at $Q_{\text{be}}$ covers fixed cost: $\text{TotalContribution}(Q_{\text{be}}) \ge F$. (As documented in Decision 1, theoretical configurations such as `per_total` + `round_half_even` can under-cover, so universal coverage is not claimed).
3. **Minimum Whole-Unit Optimality for `per_unit`:** Under `per_unit` where unit contribution is an integer minor unit, selling one fewer unit ($Q_{\text{be}} - 1$) strictly fails to cover fixed cost: $\text{TotalContribution}(Q_{\text{be}} - 1) < F$. (Under `per_total`, non-monotonic rounding means $Q_{\text{be}} - 1$ does not establish optimality).
4. **Deterministic Invariance:** Repeated runs with identical inputs produce identical JSON strings.
5. **Revenue Alignment:** For all reachable cases, $\text{revenue.amountMinor} === \text{listPriceMinor} \times Q_{\text{be}}$.

All property tests run with fixed seeds, ensuring reproducible test execution across CI pipelines.
